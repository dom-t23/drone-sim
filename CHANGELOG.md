## 2026-10-24: TV mode: a race director, trackside cameras and slow-motion replays
- **New camera: TV, the race filmed like a broadcast.** Press **Camera** (or `C`) until it says TV, or open `#seed=1&d=normal&field=1&cam=tv`; **Watch a race** on the intro card now starts in it. An automatic director cuts every few seconds between a **trackside camera** at each gate (a long lens that pans with the drone and zooms to keep it the same size in frame), a **low flyby** camera dropped beside the line so the drone whips past the lens, a tight **chase cam**, a **heli cam** and the **onboard** camera.
- **It films the racing, not just the leader.** At each cut it picks who to show: two drones within a few metres of each other on the course are a battle and get most of the airtime, the leader gets more than the back markers, and a drone that has just crashed jumps to the top. Tap a name in the timing tower to keep every shot on that drone (tap it again to hand back to the director).
- **Slow-motion replays.** A crash, a change of lead or the chequered flag rolls a replay: a REPLAY wipe, letterbox bars, the race paused, and the last few seconds flown again at 0.4× from the trackside camera with the best view of the moment. Flying solo, every second lap replays that lap's fastest gate. **Skip replay** (or `Esc`) goes straight back to live.
- **Broadcast graphics**: a LIVE / REPLAY bug with the camera's name and a lower third for the drone in shot (its colour, position, lap and speed). On a phone the shots are re-framed for the tall screen so the long lens doesn't crop the drone.
- How it works: `src/director.js` (no DOM) places one camera post per gate when TV mode starts: about 90 candidate spots around each gate (before and after it, either side, low and high) are tried, any within 1.2 m of a frame, leg or pillar or 4.5 m of the racing line is thrown out, and the one that sees the most of the line from 30 m before the gate to 10 m after it wins. Line of sight is a ray cast (slab test) against the same boxes the pixel camera draws. Each frame it works out how far round the course every drone is; at a cut it chooses a drone by interest and a shot type at random (never the same moving shot twice running), and prefers a trackside post the drone is flying towards, taking one only if it can see both where it is and where it will be 1.5 s later. A trackside shot locks onto the drone with a little lead room that eases in and out as it speeds up and brakes, sets the zoom from the distance, and cuts away early if a frame or pillar blocks the view for a third of a second. Chase and heli cams ride critically damped springs that aim ahead by exactly the spring's lag, so they keep up at 4× speed. The replay buffer keeps every drone's pose 30 times a second for 12 s and interpolates between samples (heading the short way round, the crash tumble included).
- Honest numbers: in 18 directed five-drone races (easy, normal and hard, six seeds each, 60 s), the drone was inside the picture in every trackside frame and in clear view in 96 to 98% of them (the rest is a frame or a pillar sweeping past before the cut). On 120 courses every gate got a post that sees all of its stretch of line. Placing the posts takes about 70 ms on the workspace, once, when TV mode starts; directing costs well under 0.1 ms a frame.
- Tests (`test/director.test.mjs`): line of sight through a gate opening is clear and through a bar or a pillar is blocked; on 12 courses every gate gets a post that stands clear of everything and the line and sees at least 80% of its stretch; two directed 90 s field races use every shot type, film several drones, keep shots within their length limits (shorter only when the view was lost) and keep the drone in frame over 99% and in clear view over 90% of trackside frames; the same at 4× speed on a 10 fps device; pinning holds every shot on one drone and the same seed directs the same race; the replay buffer's rate, length, interpolation, tumble and windows; and replay shots of every gate on a hard course come from a clear camera that keeps the drone in frame. The quick-start test now accepts `cam=tv`.

## 2026-10-22: Eyes: pixels: the drone finds the gates in its own camera picture
- **New Eyes button (or `E`): pixels.** With Nav: vision or vision + IMU, switch the camera from the modelled corner detector to real pixels. The onboard inset becomes the **drone's eye**: the actual 320×180 picture it works from, grainy and low-res, with green quads on the gates it found (numbered) and dashed cyan outlines where its map said they would be. Watch the dashed outline snap onto the real gate as it flies. Try **Find its way** on the intro card, or `#seed=3&d=hard&nav=vio&eyes=px`.
- **Nobody tells it where the gates are any more.** Until now the camera took the true gate corners and added noise. Now every camera frame is drawn by a small software renderer (sky, textured ground, orange gate frames with depth and shading, legs and pillars, a depth buffer so things really hide each other, sensor noise), and the detector only sees those pixels. It picks out the orange, groups it into blobs, fits a quadrilateral to each blob, refines each side by a line fit (dropping stray points, then fitting again) to sub-pixel corners (about half a pixel out, typically), and checks it really is a gate frame: a ring with straight, solid sides and nothing else tangled in it.
- **Gates line up one behind another, so their frames often touch in the picture.** A near gate with a far one poking out from behind it makes one blob that isn't a quad. The detector then takes it apart by sliding the outline it expects to see over the blob until the near gate's frame fits, and checks that like any other quad.
- **It works out which gate is which (data association).** It matches each quad to the gate it expects there, mostly by size: a map error can move a gate metres sideways, right behind another one in the picture, but barely changes how big it looks. Quads two gates could equally be are left out. Across more than 23,000 quads in test flights it never put one to the wrong gate.
- **It races as fast as before.** Second laps on pixels are within half a percent of the corner model's. It sees less far (gates are found out to about 35 m, against the model's 50 m), so on its first lap it crosses any gate it isn't sure of at the believed centre rather than where the shaped racing line would, and only cuts the corner once it has pinned the gate down. In a camera blackout it stops 11 m short of a gate it hasn't seen instead of 8 m, because the camera, tilted up like a real FPV camera, loses the bottom of a gate below it from closer in.
- In 240 test flights (30 hard courses, calm and gusty, with vision and with vision + IMU, plus 120 on easy and normal courses) it flew 237 cleanly, and in 72 camera blackouts on the first lap it never missed a gate or crashed. The corner model still does a little better on hard courses (it flew all 120 of those hard flights cleanly): two of the three slips are the same gate on one hard course, which sits right behind the next one in the picture and only comes apart about 15 m out, too late to follow the change of line cleanly.
- It costs about 3 ms of JavaScript per camera frame (30 a second), so it's off by default; the corner model is still there.
- How it works: `src/eye.js` (no DOM) holds the renderer (`renderView`: per-pixel sky and ground in 2×2 blocks, then every box face clipped to the near plane and filled with a depth test, then noise from a fixed table), the detector (`detectGates`: colour threshold, 8-connected flood fill, convex-hull rough corners, two rounds of side line fits on the outermost edge pixels, then `verify`: inside the picture, convex, solid just inside every side, clear just outside, an empty opening for the blob's own pixels, a ring's fill, and for small far quads, nothing else within a few pixels), the blob splitter (a coarse-then-fine search over shifts and three scales of each predicted outline, scored on frame just inside and clear just outside) and `associate` (corner distance as a fraction of the predicted size plus twice the log size ratio, greedy, ambiguous quads dropped). In `src/sim.js` the vision frame renders from where the drone really is, predicts each believed gate's outline from where it thinks it is, detects, associates, then fits the gate by the same PnP as before but to the face of the frame nearest the camera (half a bar in front of the gate's centre plane; fitting the centre plane read every gate 13 cm too near). Its fits go through the same gating and fusion (or the EKF-SLAM update). `shapeFor()` scales each gate's crossing offset down as the drone's doubt about it rises from 0.15 m to 0.4 m, and never swings the line further out for the next gate once it is within 25 m (a late change of line is harder to follow than a slightly slower crossing).
- Tests (`test/eye.test.mjs`, `test/eye-blackout.test.mjs`): a gate straight ahead is drawn in orange exactly where it projects and nothing else looks orange; it is found to within a pixel, turned or not; a pillar hides part of a frame and the half-hidden frame isn't taken for a gate, and a gate behind a wall or behind the camera isn't drawn at all; a far gate overlapping a near one isn't mistaken for a gate on its own, and with a (shifted, resized) prediction the near one comes out; association picks by size, drops ambiguous quads and returns corners in the predicted order; vision flies 14 easy, normal and hard courses and vision + IMU 6 more without a miss or crash, with every quad checked against every gate's true outline (none given to the wrong gate, median corner error under 0.8 px), gate estimates within 8 to 12 cm on average, the position estimate within 0.45 m, and laps no slower than the corner model's; three gusty flights; 24 first-lap blackouts; deterministic flights; and pixels only where there's a camera.

## 2026-10-14: Camera blackout: the drone knows what it doesn't know
- **New Blackout button (or `B`): cut the drone's camera for 4 seconds.** It appears whenever the drone you're watching has a camera (Nav: vision or vision + IMU, or the Vision drone in a Field race). The onboard view goes to static with **NO SIGNAL** and a countdown. Try it on the first lap of `#seed=3&nav=vio`, then again on the second.
- **On the first lap it stops short.** It hasn't pinned the gates down yet (the map has them up to 2.6 m out), so it won't fly at one blind: it brakes to a hover 8 m short of where it thinks the gate is (`Can't see gate 7: stopping short`), **turns to look at it**, and once the camera is back and finds the gate, re-plans through where it really is and sets off (`Found gate 7: go!`). It looks past the next gate too, so a doubtful gate straight after a known one doesn't catch it at full speed. If it's already too close to stop well short, it commits and flies through on what it has.
- **On a mapped lap it races straight through the dark.** In vision mode it knows where every gate is to a few centimetres by then, so it doesn't even lift. Lap times barely change.
- **With vision + IMU you can watch it lose itself.** In the dark it flies on the accelerometer alone, so the magenta uncertainty ball grows (to over twice its size), and after a couple of seconds it's unsure enough of *itself* that even a known gate is in doubt, so it stops for that too. The first gate it sees snaps the estimate back to within a few centimetres.
- Without the caution the same first-lap blackouts crash a lot: 11 crashes in 24 test flights on normal courses, against none with it. Over about 1,100 trial blackouts (every difficulty, both camera modes, calm and gusty, first and second laps) it missed no gates and crashed once.
- How it works: `cameraBlackout()` in `src/sim.js` stops the detector for a few seconds. `gateDoubt()` gives how sure the drone is of each gate *relative to itself*: the 1-sigma size of the gate's covariance, and with vision + IMU the joint covariance of gate minus drone from the EKF-SLAM state (so a drone unsure of itself doubts every gate). `blindCap()` finds the first gate within stopping reach whose doubt is over 0.2 m, and caps the racing pilot's speed so it can stop 8 m short at 6 m/s² (braking limited to twice that, so the steering never starves). Once it commits to a stop it holds until the camera pins the gate down (or 3 s after the camera is back, if it still can't find it), then re-plans and relaunches gently, like after a respawn. The line is also re-planned through the latest estimates the moment the camera goes dark. Nothing changes until you press the button.
- Tests: the camera really goes dark (no detections, no frames, gate estimates frozen) and comes back; no camera, nothing to cut; unseen gates are doubtful and mapped ones aren't; first-lap blackouts at three points on every difficulty, 4 seeds each, for vision and for vision + IMU, with no misses and no crashes, every hold staying over 4 m from the real gate, most of them coming to a full stop and all finding the gate again; the same blackouts on normal courses crash at least 3 times without the caution; a vision blackout on a mapped lap never stops and still beats the first lap; a vision + IMU blackout on a mapped lap grows the uncertainty at least twofold, then shrinks it by half and brings the error back under 0.4 m; and blackout flights are deterministic.

## 2026-10-10 (second run): Real races: racecraft, contact and a chequered flag
- **Field races now have an ending.** A race is three laps, shown as `Race · lap 2/3` in the timing tower. When the winner completes lap 3 the **chequered flag** comes out (a toast and a chime); everyone else finishes the next time they cross the line, F1 style, and the tower freezes them in finishing order with a chequered marker. When the last drone is home a **results card** pops up: winner, race time, gaps (or `+1 lap`), best laps, crash counts and how many times they touched, with **Race again**, **Next course** and **Keep watching** buttons. The drones fly on behind it. Try `#seed=1&field=1`: Racing line wins in 0:50.15, Vision 1.41 s behind.
- **The drones are solid to each other.** Until now they flew through each other like ghosts. Now two drones that touch bounce apart (sparks and a "Contact!" call), and a hit faster than 6 m/s takes **both** out, with the same tumble and respawn as hitting a gate. With **Pilot: you** in a Field race, the autopilots are solid to you too, so you can block, get bumped, or take someone out.
- **Racecraft.** Every autopilot now sees the other drones and looks 1.2 s ahead for anyone it's about to meet. It **steps its line sideways to pass** (on the inside of the turn when someone is dead ahead), squeezing back onto the line before every gate so an overtake never costs a frame. If a slower drone is still in the way it **holds back** like adaptive cruise control, never closing faster than it could stop 1.5 m behind. The Pursuit drone, which gets lapped, **moves aside** for the leaders. Watch the start: five drones funnel into the first gate and sort themselves into a queue, and Racing line and Vision now fly side by side or nose to tail instead of sitting inside each other.
- Racecraft cuts contacts by about three quarters (21 versus 73 over nine test races) and drone-on-drone crashes from 7 in 24 one-minute races to 2, with no missed gates. Lap times are unchanged outside traffic.
- A bump shows up in a vision + IMU drone's accelerometer, as it would for real, so its position estimate takes the jolt with it instead of being knocked 3 m/s out.
- How it works: `bump()` in `src/sim.js` treats drones as 0.3 m spheres of equal mass: it separates them and applies an impulse with 0.4 restitution, or starts a crash for both above 6 m/s. Drones on the pad, tumbling or holding at a respawn point are left out, so nobody respawns into a crash. `shareTraffic()` hands each autopilot the others' positions and velocities; the racing pilot finds each one's closest approach over the next 1.2 s and shifts its reference point square to the line, away from it, with the room to move shrinking from 2 m in the open to 0.3 m at a gate (less if it's already off the line). The hold-back caps the reference speed at the other drone's speed plus √(2·8·(gap − 1.5 m)), limited to 8 m/s² of braking so it never starves the steering. The race logic (flag, finishing order, DNFs after a 30 s timeout, gaps) lives in `src/field.js`, with no DOM.
- Tests: a gentle bump separates two drones, keeps their momentum and trades speed without a crash; a hard one crashes both and they respawn; drones on the pad or respawning are ghosts; a vision + IMU drone's estimate follows the jolt (it misses by 2.8 m/s without the accelerometer fix); sharing traffic with nobody changes nothing; six full three-lap field races on easy, normal and hard finish with no misses, the optimised line (with or without vision) winning, Pursuit lapped and classified last, times in order, at most 2 crashes and less than half the contacts of the same races without racecraft; a gusty two-lap race finishes cleanly; and the flag, late finishers, lapped finishers, DNFs and gaps, with hand-made events.

## 2026-10-10: Vision + IMU: the drone has to work out where it is
- **New Nav mode: vision + IMU** (press **Nav** or `V` until it says so, or open `#seed=3&d=hard&nav=vio`). Until now the drone always knew exactly where it was; only the gates were uncertain. Now it doesn't. All it has is its camera and an **accelerometer**, with noise and an unknown bias that is different on every drone and slowly wanders. It still flies every course cleanly, no misses and no crashes, about as fast as with perfect knowledge.
- **In the chase view**, a **magenta wire ball** shows where the drone thinks it is, sized to its uncertainty (two sigma), joined to the real drone by a line. A **red ball** shows where the accelerometer alone would put it: it starts each lap on the drone and wanders several metres off by the end of the lap. That's what the camera is saving it from. The cyan gates still show where it thinks each gate is.
- A new **Position** row in the stats shows how far out the estimate is right now and how far the IMU alone has drifted (e.g. `0.14 m · IMU 2.8 m`). **Telemetry** gets a **Position error** chart, plotted against the filter's own 2σ bound (dashed): a healthy filter stays mostly under it, and this one does.
- It sits on the pad for a second before launch. While it's held still it knows it isn't moving, and that's enough to measure the accelerometer bias (from up to 0.3 m/s² down to about 0.03).
- How it works: an **extended Kalman filter** (EKF-SLAM) whose state holds the drone's position, velocity and accelerometer bias **and every gate's position**, 45 numbers on a 12-gate course. Each accelerometer reading (120 a second) moves the estimate forward and grows the uncertainty. Each gate the camera finds becomes a measurement of where that gate is *relative to the drone*, which corrects both at once, so the drone and the map are solved together. Because the filter tracks how the drone's error and each gate's error are linked, seeing a gate again after a lap pulls the whole loop back into line (loop closure). On the pad, zero-velocity updates find the bias. The same innovation gate as vision mode throws out wild detections (all of them in testing, with no clean ones lost). The wind observer now runs on the accelerometer and the estimated velocity, and after a crash the estimate restarts near the respawn point. Attitude is still taken as known, as if from a good gyro; estimating that is a next step.
- Honest numbers: the absolute position estimate averages about 0.2 m out (the whole map and the drone can drift a little together, which is the classic SLAM gauge problem), but relative to the gate it's about to fly through it's about 6 cm, which is what steering needs. Laps cost about 1% against ground truth on average (between 1% faster and 3% slower, course by course).
- Ground truth, vision and map-only flights are unchanged.
- Tests: the new mode exists and the others carry no filter; the body frame is right-handed and orthonormal; zero-velocity updates cut the bias error at least fivefold before launch; easy, normal and hard courses flown on 8 seeds each with no misses or crashes, position error under 1.2 m at worst and 0.45 m on average, relative-to-gate error under 0.12 m on average, the estimate outside its 3σ bound under 2% of the time, the IMU alone straying over 2 m, and the bias still found at the end; clean in gusty wind; laps within 3.5% of ground truth on average; recovery after a crash; and determinism.

## 2026-10-09: Night mode, prop wash and motor sound
- **New Night button (or `L`): race after dark.** The sky goes black and fills with stars and a moon, and every gate lights up like an LED race gate: cool blue when waiting, **orange for the next gate** (which also throws real orange light onto the ground, its legs and the drone as you fly through), green once passed. Each gate glows softly and casts a pool of coloured light on the ground beneath it. The drone you're watching carries a **headlight** that sweeps the ground ahead, so the onboard camera is a proper night FPV view.
- **The drones have LEDs**: red and green at the front, blue at the back, so you can tell which way they're facing in the dark (and by day).
- **Prop wash.** The air the props blow down is now visible as faint wisps, and when a drone flies or hovers low (try **Pilot: you**, or watch the start of a **Field** race on the grid) it kicks up a ring of dust that spreads out across the ground. Wind blows it downwind, and a fast drone leaves it behind.
- **New Sound button (or `M`): motor sound.** A synthesised four-motor whine whose pitch follows the rotor speed (pinned at hover, screaming at full throttle), a rush of air that grows with speed, a blip at every gate, a chime at the line and a crunch when you crash. It follows whichever drone you're watching. Off by default; it goes quiet when the tab is hidden.
- Links work too: `#seed=3&d=hard&field=1&night=1`.
- How it works: the new `src/fx.js` holds the physics behind the effects, with no rendering code. Rotor speed goes as the square root of thrust, so the motor pitch does too. The wash leaves the props at the **induced velocity** from actuator-disc momentum theory (T = 2ρAv², about 7 m/s at hover for a 5-inch quad), slows as it mixes, and on reaching the ground turns outward into a dust ring. `src/audio.js` builds the sound from Web Audio oscillators and filtered noise, so there are no audio files to download. The night lights are switched off by day, so day mode keeps its old speed.
- Tests: induced velocity is about 7 m/s at hover and grows as the root of thrust; the motor tone sits at its hover pitch, rises with thrust and cuts out in a crash; wash from a low hover becomes a dust ring spreading at least 2.5 m out that never sinks through the ground, while wash from 15 m up never reaches it; no wash in a crash; a fast drone's wash trails behind it and wind carries it downwind; and everything stays finite through 40 s of gusty flight on a hard course.

## 2026-10-07: Crashes
- **Gates and pillars are solid now.** Clip a gate frame, a support leg or one of the scenery pillars and the drone cuts out: it bounces off, tumbles to the ground in a shower of sparks, and a red toast says what you hit (`Crashed into gate 4`). After about a second and a half it **respawns on the racing line just past the last gate you passed**, hovers for half a second and is yours again. A crash costs time, never progress, and you can't crash your way to a shortcut. The **Gates** row counts crashes (`7 · 2 crashed`).
- Try it with **Pilot: you**: the bottom bar of a low gate is easy to catch if you hold descend. Light touches (under 1.5 m/s into the surface) don't count: the drone just slides along the frame.

It now predicts where it will cross each gate, goes round for another try if that's off the opening (swinging wide of the frame), and eases off when it's off the gate's axis close in. On 40 hard seeds with ground truth it laps without a crash (with vision, the odd one).
- Hard and easy courses whose last leg is short now start the drone just past the last gate rather than right in front of its frame.
- The scenery pillars moved into the simulation (`makeScenery`) so they can be hit. Same layout as before, minus any pillar within 7 m of the course.
- Tests: the gate opening is clear wherever the drone fits through it, and bars, legs and pillars are solid with sensible push-out directions; pillars stay at least 4 m from every racing line; a head-on hit tumbles to the ground and respawns at the start with a hover before the sticks come back; a crash later in the lap respawns just past the last gate passed and the lap can still be finished; a gentle touch slides instead of crashing; crash flights are deterministic. ## 2026-10-05: You fly
- **Pilot: you.** The Pilot button (or `P`) now has a third setting: you fly the drone yourself and race the racing-line autopilot, which flies the same course at the same time as a **translucent cyan ghost**. Both sit on the pad through a **3, 2, 1, GO!** countdown. A new **Rival** row in the HUD shows the gap at the last gate you've both passed (red `+2.31 s` when you're behind, green `−0.40 s` when you're ahead). Lap toasts compare you with the rival's best and call a **new best**. Personal bests are kept per course in your browser. Links work too: `#seed=2&pilot=manual` sends a friend straight into a race.
- **Controls**: `W`/`↑` for speed, `A` `D`/`←` `→` to turn, `Space`/`Shift` (or `E`/`Q`) to climb and descend. On a phone, two on-screen sticks appear: speed and turn on the left, climb and drift on the right. A gamepad works as well (left stick: speed and turn; right stick: climb and drift).
- It flies like a camera drone in "velocity mode". The sticks ask for a forward speed (up to 94 km/h), a turn rate, a climb rate and a sideways drift. The same thrust-vector inner loop the autopilot uses delivers them, inside the airframe's tilt and thrust limits. Turn rate is capped by speed so the drone can always follow its nose. When your climb stick is centred, **altitude assist** eases you to the next gate's height, and the drone won't descend below 1.2 m.

A scripted stick-only pilot that uses nothing but the sticks has to fly every easy and normal test course with no misses (about 28 s a lap; the autopilot does about 16 s). ## 2026-10-04 (fourth run): Wind
- **New Wind button (or `W`)**: off, **breezy** (6 m/s with 2.5 m/s gusts) or **gusty** (12 m/s with 6 m/s gusts), from a random direction per course and saved in the URL (`&wind=gusty`). Streaks drift past the drone so you can see it blowing, and a new **Wind** HUD row shows the real wind next to the drone's own estimate (e.g. `19 m/s · est 19`).
- The drone measures the wind it's fighting with a **disturbance observer**: it compares the acceleration it actually gets with what its still-air model predicted, low-passes the difference, and cancels it in the next command. Over a flight its average wind estimate matches the real wind to within 0.5 m/s. In gusty wind it cuts the worst tracking errors by about a third; on hard courses, the worst error from the line drops from 1.9 m to 1.1 m.
- **Planning for the forecast**: when wind is forecast, the racing line keeps back the share of the drone's thrust the wind can take (drag at the mean wind plus two gust sigmas), so there is always thrust left to fight a gust. Gusty laps are about 1.5 s slower, but nothing is missed: zero misses on 60 seeds per difficulty, in gusty wind, with ground truth or vision.
- Gusts are modelled as correlated random processes (1.5 s correlation time, weaker vertically), capped at two sigma.
- On easy and hard courses the drone now starts hovering on the racing line 18 m before the start gate, instead of on the ground beneath it. That had meant climbing up to 7 m straight off the pad, which a gust could turn into a miss.
- Tests: gusty wind on every difficulty with no misses; the observer's estimate is accurate and it cuts worst-case tracking error by at least 15%; gusts stay within their cap; calm runs carry no wind.

## 2026-10-04 (third run): A shaped racing line
- **Faster laps everywhere**: the racing line no longer aims at the dead centre of every gate. It now cuts each gate up to 0.6 m off centre, toward the inside of the turn, and chooses how hard to swing into each one. Normal laps drop from about 17.2 s to **16.3 s**, easy from 14.1 s to 13.6 s and hard from 20.8 s to 20.1 s, still with no missed gates on 40 seeds of each difficulty. Watch the line on the course clip the inside of the gates.
- How: for every gate, three numbers (sideways and vertical crossing offset, and tangent length) are tuned by coordinate descent on the planned lap time, using quick coarse plans with shrinking steps (about 150 ms per course). Lines that dip within 1.8 m of the ground are refused. The start gate keeps its centre line because it's crossed mid-launch.
- Vision mode shapes the line for its (wrong) map at first. Once it has seen every gate (after lap 1) it re-shapes the line for the corrected course, so it ends up within about 1–2% of ground-truth lap times.
- Tests: gates are now judged against the planned crossing point (within 0.75 m) and must still clear the frame by 0.35 m. Crossings stay inside their limits and the start gate stays centred. Shaping must never make a plan slower, and must gain at least 4.5% on normal courses. Lap bars tighten: normal from 17.9 s to 17.1 s, hard from 22.5 s to 21 s.

## 2026-10-04 (second run): Vision mode
- **New Nav button (or `V`)** with three modes. **Ground truth** is how it has always flown: the drone knows exactly where every gate is. In **vision** and **map only**, the drone's map of the course is wrong: every gate has been moved up to 2.6 m and turned by up to 5° since it was surveyed.
- **Map only** trusts that map and misses gate after gate. You can watch it fly cleanly through the cyan "ghost" gate where it thinks the gate is, right past the real one. The HUD counts the misses and a red toast calls each one.
- **Vision** gets a camera. Before launch it looks at the first gate for half a second; then, every frame (30 per second), it finds the corners of the real gates it can see. The onboard view shows them as green boxes (red when a detection is thrown out), and the cyan ghosts slide onto the real gates as the estimates settle. The racing line is re-planned through the corrected gates in flight, and the line on the course redraws as it changes. A new **Vision** row in the HUD shows how far out the map was for the next gate and how far out the estimate is now (e.g. `2.3 → 0.04 m`). It flies every test course on every difficulty with no misses, about as fast as with perfect knowledge.
- How it works: the camera in `sim.js` is the rendered onboard camera, checked against three.js to 10⁻¹² of a pixel. It adds 1 px of noise to every corner, now and then loses a corner (standing in for occlusion and blur), and 3% of the time gets one corner 30 px wrong. Each detection becomes a gate pose by **PnP**: Levenberg-Marquardt on the reprojection error, with a weak prior on orientation so far-off gates stay well posed. Bad fits are dropped on their misfit, then an **innovation gate** (chi-square, 99.9%) checks each measurement against what the drone already believes. Survivors are fused into each gate's estimate with an information filter. Wild detections are rejected essentially every time; clean ones almost never. The drone's own position is still taken as known; estimating that is step 2.
- The racing-line planner is about 4× faster (11 ms → under 3 ms a plan), with identical output, so re-planning in flight is cheap. Ground-truth flights are bit-for-bit unchanged.
- On phones, the stats panel no longer wraps, and the onboard picture-in-picture is a little bigger and sits above the buttons instead of behind them.
- Tests: the camera model and PnP, map-only flights missing gates, vision flying every difficulty with no misses (within 1.2 m of every gate centre, with estimates under 0.5 m out at every gate), outlier rejection, lap time within 3% of ground truth, and determinism.

## 2026-10-03: Racing line
- **The drone is about 35% faster**: laps drop from about 27.3 s to about 17.6 s on every test seed, still with no missed gates. Top speed goes from 45 km/h to about 80 km/h.
- The course now shows the **planned racing line**, coloured by planned speed: blue where the drone brakes for a turn, orange where it's flat out.
- New **Pilot** button (or `P`) switches between the new racing-line autopilot and the original axis pursuit, so you can watch the difference on the same course. The HUD's new **Plan** row shows the lap time the planner predicts.
- How it works: a closed cubic Hermite spline through every gate centre, crossing each gate square-on; a speed profile that keeps each turn inside the airframe's tilt and thrust envelope (diving turns get the least, since less vertical thrust means less sideways force at max tilt), with forward/backward passes for acceleration and braking limits; and a tracker that adds the path's centripetal, along-track and drag feedforward to PD feedback, looking ahead to cover the thrust lag.
- Tests now fly both pilots, lock in the lap-time gain (racing line under 18.6 s and at least 25% faster than pursuit), check the drone threads every gate within 0.8 m of centre and stays within 1.2 m of its line, and check the plan never asks for more tilt or thrust than the drone has.
